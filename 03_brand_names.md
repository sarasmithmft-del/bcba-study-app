# 03 — Brand Name Candidates (v3 — Trademark + Commercial Yield)

You asked for names that are **(a) trademarkable** and **(b) likely to yield strong commercial results**. Those two goals usually pull in opposite directions — easy-to-understand names are usually too generic to defend as trademarks, and strong trademarks usually feel "made up." The sweet spot is a **coined or arbitrary word** that *suggests* meaning without literally describing the service.

This document is built around that sweet spot.

---

## Quick Primer: What Actually Trademarks

The USPTO ranks mark strength on a spectrum. From weakest to strongest:

| Strength | Type | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weakest | **Generic** | "Hygiene Consulting" | **Cannot** be trademarked |
| Weak | **Descriptive** | "Dental Practice Growth" | Only with proof of "acquired distinctiveness" — years and $$ |
| Medium | **Suggestive** | "The Sacred Chair Co." | Trademarkable, decent protection |
| Strong | **Arbitrary** | "Apple" (for computers) | Strong protection |
| Strongest | **Fanciful / Coined** | "Kodak," "Xerox" | **Easiest to defend, hardest to lose** |

**Translation for your brand**: aim for a coined or arbitrary word. Adding "Co.", "Group", or "Method" doesn't hurt trademark strength but doesn't help either — the *root word* is what gets protected.

You'll be filing in:
- **Class 35** (business consulting / training services for dental practices)
- **Class 41** (educational services / publications / CE)
- **Class 16** (printed materials — the book, workbooks)

Budget: ~$350 per class for DIY USPTO filing; $1,500–3,000 with a trademark attorney (recommended).

## How to Verify Availability (do this BEFORE you commit)

For your top pick, run these checks before announcing the name anywhere:

1. **USPTO TESS Search** (free): https://tmsearch.uspto.gov — search the root word and variants in Classes 35, 41, and 16.
2. **Common Law Search**: Google the exact phrase + "dental." Check Instagram, LinkedIn, podcast directories, Amazon book titles.
3. **Domain Search**: Check `.com` (must have) and `.co` (backup). Premium-broker pricing is usually worth it for a coined word that nobody else has thought of yet.
4. **Social handles**: Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube, X — same handle on all five is the goal.
5. **Trademark Attorney Review**: Once you've narrowed to 1–2, spend $400–$800 with a TM attorney for a formal availability opinion. This is the single best money you'll spend on the brand.

# Addendum: Acronym Options (A.P.E.S. / A.P.E. / A.P.E.X.)

You asked about acronym-based names starting with A.P.E. Here is my honest take, plus all the options.

## The Perception Flag (Read First)

`A.P.E.S.` spells **"apes."** `A.P.E.` spells **"ape."** Even with periods, the first thing the eye sees is the word, not the acronym. In a clinical, conservative industry where the buyer is often a 50-year-old practice owner writing a $25,000 consulting check, the word association is a risk. It may read as bold and memorable (Bumble, Geico, Snapchat all use animal/word associations successfully) OR as unprofessional. It will polarize.

## My Recommendation: Use A.P.E.S. As Your METHODOLOGY, Not Your BRAND

A methodology can have any memorable acronym (S.O.A.P. notes, S.W.A.T., D.A.R.E., M.A.S.H. all spell loaded words and work fine professionally). A brand is what doctors see first on a contract and a website. Keep them separate.

The Two-Methodology Architecture:

```
                      [BRAND COMPANY]
                  (e.g., Chairnomics™)
                          │
        ┌─────────────────┴──────────────────┐
        │                                    │
   THE C.H.A.I.R. METHOD™            THE A.P.E.S. METHOD™
   (chairside / clinical)             (practice-wide / consulting)
   What hygienists do in              What Sara does with a
   the 50-minute appointment          practice over an engagement
                                      
    C — Connect                        A — Audit
    H — Hear & Hunt                    P — Plan
    A — Assess & Articulate            E — Engage
    I — Invite                         S — Sustain
    R — Reinforce & Re-Engage
```

This is elegant. Two methodologies, two levels, both yours, both trademarkable, both billable as separate courses/certifications.

## A.P.E.S. Methodology — Recommended Meaning

**A.P.E.S. = Audit. Plan. Engage. Sustain.**

The four phases of a Chairnomics consulting engagement:

| Phase | What It Is | What You Deliver |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Audit** | Measure the practice using the 3 Revenue Streams (Ch 2) + Hygiene Health Score™ (Ch 11) | The Hygiene Health Score™ Report |
| **P — Plan** | Build the customized C.H.A.I.R. rollout plan + Double Hygiene Decision Matrix™ output + staffing math | The Practice Plan document |
| **E — Engage** | Train the hygienists, choreograph the doctor handoffs, install scripts, plant workbook tools live in the operatory | Operatory training + role-play workshops |
| **S — Sustain** | The Mentor Cadence™ (Ch 12) — Daily Reset, Weekly Walk-Through, Monthly Mentor Hour, Quarterly Quiet | Ongoing retainer + accountability rhythm |

This is your **billable consulting product.** Practices pay for an A.P.E.S. engagement. The certification you offer downstream is *"A.P.E.S. Certified Implementer."* The methodology is the business model.

## A.P.E.X. — The Stronger Brand Option

If you want the acronym to *be* your brand (not just methodology), go one letter further. **A.P.E.X. spells "Apex"** — peak, summit, highest point. Strong commercial word, authoritative, scalable.

Possible meanings:
- **A.P.E.X. = Audit. Plan. Engage. eXecute.**
- **A.P.E.X. = Aligned Practice EXcellence**
- **A.P.E.X. = Authority. Practice. Elevation. eXpansion.**

> ⚠️ **TM caveat**: "Apex Dental" practices exist in many states; "Apex Practice Consulting" may exist too. USPTO TESS search in Classes 35/41 is mandatory before committing. If it clears, A.P.E.X. is *significantly* stronger as a brand mark than A.P.E.S. because "apex" is a universally positive word and "apes" is not.
