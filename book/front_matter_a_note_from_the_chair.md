# A Note From The Chair

If you are holding this book, you already know something most of dentistry still treats like a side conversation:

the hygiene chair is not a cleaning station.
It is the most underleveraged growth engine in the practice.

I wrote this as an experienced registered dental hygienist with a unique background in psychology and behavior, and as someone who has implemented successful hygiene systems that increased production and revenue across many settings.

Not by selling.
By building better systems, stronger communication, and consistent follow-through.

Across offices of every size, I kept seeing the same pattern:

Good clinicians under-mentored.
Strong findings under-articulated.
Treatment opportunities under-converted.
Retention value under-protected.
Hygiene teams under-credited for the growth they quietly drive.

This book exists to fix that pattern.

Not with hype.
Not with scripts that feel salesy.
Not with another "be more efficient" speech that ignores team stamina.

With a system.

The C.H.A.I.R. Method gives you a repeatable choreography for what happens inside the appointment:

- Connect
- Hear & Hunt
- Assess & Articulate
- Invite
- Reinforce & Re-Engage

And the A.P.E.S. Method gives you a repeatable implementation and leadership framework for installing that choreography in a real practice:

- Assess
- Plan
- Engage
- Sustain

If you are a hygienist, this book is your evidence that your role is larger than what most systems have allowed.
If you are a doctor, this book is your blueprint for turning hygiene from overhead conversation to strategic advantage.
If you are an office leader, this book is an implementation map you can run.

You will notice three things as you read:

1. The language is direct.
   - Because vague language is where growth leaks.
2. The tools are practical.
   - Because ideas do not change practices; behaviors do.
3. The tone is accountable.
   - Because patients deserve care that is both human and measurable.

I do not believe in choosing between care and growth.
I believe growth is what happens when care is designed well, communicated clearly, and followed through consistently.

That is Chairnomics.
That is this book.

You are not here to read and nod.
You are here to implement.

So bring this to huddle.
Mark it up.
Test one tool this week.
Teach one framework to your team.
Track what changes.

The chair is golden ground.
Let's do work worthy of it.

— Sara Smith, RDH  
Founder, Chairnomics
