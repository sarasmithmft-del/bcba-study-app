# Chapter 11
## The Hygiene Health Score - Making Care Visible

> *"If you only measure production, you will optimize for speed. If you measure health, you will optimize for outcomes."*

---

## The Practice That Was "Winning" on Paper

A doctor in a three-provider office told me, with justified pride, that hygiene production was up 22% year-over-year.

He was right.
The number was real.
Collections were up. Encounters were up. Schedules were full. Team energy looked high in huddle photos. On paper, this was what winning looked like.

Then we ran an A.P.E.S. Assess phase baseline.

What surfaced under the production headline:

- HPPH up
- CCDR flat
- same-day acceptance down 9 points
- reappointment rate down 11 points
- patient "felt rushed" comments up
- doctor exam delays rising

Same office.
Same month.
Two different stories depending on what you measured.

The owner had not done anything irresponsible.
He had done what most practices do: tracked the number easiest to see.

But production alone cannot tell you whether your hygiene system is healthy.
It can only tell you whether money moved.

If you are serious about hygiene-led growth, you need a score that combines financial, behavioral, and continuity indicators into one usable signal.

That is why this chapter exists.
That is what the Hygiene Health Score is for.

---

## Why Most KPI Dashboards Fail

Most hygiene dashboards are one of two extremes:

1. **Too shallow**: one or two metrics (usually production and encounters)
2. **Too complex**: 20+ metrics no one can act on

Both fail for the same reason:
they do not support decisions.

A useful score system should do four things:

1. detect risk early
2. guide weekly action
3. support quarterly strategy
4. compare trends over time

The Hygiene Health Score is designed for this exact job.

It is not a replacement for your full dashboard.
It is the "headline indicator" that tells leadership whether to drill deeper and where.

Think of it like blood pressure:

- one number does not tell the whole story
- but one number can tell you if you need immediate attention

---

## The Hygiene Health Score

The Hygiene Health Score is a 0-100 composite index built from four core inputs:

1. **HPPH** - Hygiene Production Per Hour
2. **CCDR** - Chairside Co-Diagnosis Rate
3. **RAR** - Reappointment / Reattendance Rate
4. **SDA** - Same-Day Acceptance from hygiene-originated findings

These four were chosen because together they represent:

- economic output (HPPH)
- diagnostic activation (CCDR)
- continuity and retention (RAR)
- patient decision confidence (SDA)

No single metric can stand in for all four.

---

## Score Formula (Version 1)

You can weight differently by practice model, but this baseline works well for most GP offices:

> **HHS = (0.25 x HPPH Score) + (0.25 x CCDR Score) + (0.25 x RAR Score) + (0.25 x SDA Score)**

Each component is normalized to 0-100 before weighting.

Equal weighting keeps the score balanced and easier to trust during early adoption.
You can move to custom weighting later if needed.

---

## Normalization Rules

To convert raw metrics into 0-100 component scores, use defined benchmark bands.

### 1) HPPH Component (0-100)

Use your region-adjusted fee context, but a practical baseline:

- <150 = 20
- 150-199 = 50
- 200-249 = 70
- 250-299 = 85
- >=300 = 100

### 2) CCDR Component (0-100)

- <10% = 15
- 10-19% = 40
- 20-29% = 60
- 30-39% = 80
- >=40% = 100

### 3) RAR Component (0-100)

- <70% = 20
- 70-79% = 50
- 80-87% = 75
- 88-92% = 90
- >=93% = 100

### 4) SDA Component (0-100)

- <25% = 20
- 25-34% = 45
- 35-44% = 65
- 45-54% = 85
- >=55% = 100

These are practical implementation benchmarks, not moral judgments.
Use trend direction as seriously as absolute score.

---

## Score Bands and Meaning

| HHS Band | Status | What It Usually Means | Priority Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **85-100** | High health | System is compounding | Protect gains, refine precision, avoid complacency |
| **70-84** | Stable but improvable | Some strong areas, one hidden leak | Identify weakest component and run 30-day sprint |
| **55-69** | At-risk | Results are fragile; variance likely | A.P.E.S. Assess + Plan intervention |
| **<55** | Unstable | Structural breakdown in one or more domains | Reset operating model before scaling |

Do not celebrate one great month in isolation.
Require 90-day trend consistency before declaring system stability.

## Example: Score Calculation in Practice

Practice baseline this month:

- HPPH = 238 -> HPPH Score = 70
- CCDR = 27% -> CCDR Score = 60
- RAR = 82% -> RAR Score = 75
- SDA = 31% -> SDA Score = 45

Apply formula:

HHS = (0.25 x 70) + (0.25 x 60) + (0.25 x 75) + (0.25 x 45)
HHS = 17.5 + 15 + 18.75 + 11.25
HHS = **62.5**

Interpretation:

- Current system is at-risk.
- Growth exists but is not compounding reliably.
- Highest leak is SDA (decision conversion).
- Primary intervention should target Chapters 6 and 7 behavior, not just schedule compression.

This is why a composite score matters.
Without it, leaders overreact to the strongest metric and ignore the weakest one.

## Do Not Hide Behind Averages

A common KPI mistake:
reviewing one office-wide average and calling it a day.

You need at least three views:

1. **Practice-level HHS**
2. **Provider-level HHS** (per hygienist)
3. **Trend-level HHS** (rolling 3 months)

Why provider-level matters:

- one high performer can mask system fragility
- one struggling column can drag doctor flow and patient experience

Why rolling trend matters:

- single-month variation is noisy
- trends show whether change is real

Rule:
Never make structural decisions from one month of data.

## Red Flag Patterns You Must Recognize

### Pattern A: "Production Up, Trust Down"

Signal:

- HPPH rising
- RAR and/or SDA falling

Meaning:
Short-term throughput is consuming relationship quality.

Fix:
Rebalance pace with Connect and Reinforce behaviors. Audit rushed-care indicators.

### Pattern B: "Great Diagnosis, Weak Conversion"

Signal:

- CCDR high
- SDA low

Meaning:
Findings are seen, but articulation or invitation is weak.

Fix:
Run Chapter 6 and 7 language sprints. Audit Bridge quality.

### Pattern C: "Stable Conversion, Weak Retention"

Signal:

- SDA stable
- RAR declining

Meaning:
Decision quality is okay, continuity quality is leaking.

Fix:
Activate Chapter 3 continuity protocol and Chapter 12 cadence.

### Pattern D: "Everything Flat"

Signal:

- all components in mid bands

Meaning:
No crisis, no compounding. Team is operationally surviving.

Fix:
Choose one component for 30-day focused lift. Avoid trying to improve all four simultaneously.

## The Monthly KPI Rhythm (What Leaders Actually Do)

A score is only as useful as the rhythm around it.

Use this cadence:

### Weekly (20-minute Walk-Through)

- review component deltas only
- identify one operational cause
- assign one owner action for next week

### Monthly (Leadership Review)

- calculate full HHS
- compare provider-level spread
- decide one monthly sprint focus

### Quarterly (Strategic Review)

- review rolling 3-month HHS
- evaluate staffing model fit (Q1-Q4 matrix from Chapter 9)
- adjust growth targets and mentorship interventions

This aligns with A.P.E.S.:

- Assess: baseline and component mapping
- Plan: priority intervention selection
- Engage: behavior/workflow execution
- Sustain: trend stability and drift correction

## Building an Honest Score Culture

Numbers can motivate or punish depending on leadership behavior.

If HHS is used as a weapon, people game it.
If HHS is used as a development instrument, people improve it.

Leadership rules:

1. Score is for system learning, not individual shaming.
2. Every low score gets support plan, not blame speech.
3. Every high score gets codified into teachable behavior.
4. Metrics are discussed with context and stories.

Never separate numbers from narrative.
That is how teams disengage from data.

---

## What to Do in the First 90 Days

If your practice has never used this score, do not over-engineer.
Use this rollout:

### Days 1-30

- define metric formulas and data sources
- calculate baseline HHS at practice and provider level
- share score purpose with team

### Days 31-60

- choose one weak component as sprint target
- run one intervention (language, handoff, continuity, or schedule)
- track weekly deltas

### Days 61-90

- compare baseline vs current
- review unintended effects (burnout, patient comments)
- lock next quarter's focus

Goal of first 90 days:
trust the score process, not chase perfect numbers.

---

## Score Integrity: Data Quality Checks

Bad data destroys confidence fast.
Use these checks monthly:

1. Are all hygiene encounters tagged to correct provider?
2. Is treatment-source attribution consistent?
3. Are reappointment completions measured the same way each month?
4. Is same-day acceptance definition fixed (not changing by manager)?
5. Are no-shows/cancels handled consistently in denominator logic?

If definitions drift, trends lie.

Create a one-page "KPI Definitions Sheet" and lock it.
No metric should depend on memory.

---

## Pairing HHS with Financial Reviews

Owners often ask:
"Do I review this with finance or clinical leadership?"

Answer: both.

Monthly review structure:

1. Financial lens (10 min)
   - production/collections context
2. Clinical lens (10 min)
   - component drivers and care quality patterns
3. Operational lens (10 min)
   - scheduling, exam flow, staffing friction
4. Mentorship lens (10 min)
   - Chapter 12 cadence adherence and burnout indicators

If any one lens is missing, action quality drops.

If you cannot summarize hygiene system health in one honest number and explain what drives that number, you are managing activity, not outcomes.

Measure what matters.
Weight it transparently.
