# Chapter 7
## I is for Invite - The Handoff That Doesn't Leak

> *"Most acceptance losses do not happen when the doctor presents options. They happen in the thirty seconds before the doctor enters the room."*

---

## The Eleven-Second Gap That Cost Six Figures

The owner thought his issue was case presentation.

He told me:

> "My doctors are excellent. Patients love them. But case acceptance from hygiene is inconsistent and unpredictable."

He was right about the unpredictability.
He was wrong about the source.

During an A.P.E.S. Assess phase, I observed two hygienists with similar patient volumes, similar finding rates, and similar doctors covering exams.

Their outcomes were not similar.

- Hygienist A: same-day acceptance from hygiene-originated findings = 49%
- Hygienist B: same-day acceptance = 22%

Clinical quality was not the differentiator.
Both were skilled.
Both were liked.
Both documented thoroughly.

The differentiator was handoff design.

Hygienist A used a structured invitation sequence before the doctor entered:

1. she pre-framed the transition
2. she named the finding clearly
3. she gave the patient agency
4. she bridged the doctor in with context

Elapsed time: about eleven seconds.

Hygienist B used a common default:

> "Doctor will take a look."

That was it.
No context. No orientation. No patient agency.

By the time the doctor entered, one patient was prepared for a decision conversation. The other was prepared for a routine exam.

Same doctor.
Same finding type.
Same day.

Different invitation.
Different outcome.

That is this chapter.

Invite is where hygienists stop handing off uncertainty and start handing off momentum.

---

## What "Invite" Actually Means

In C.H.A.I.R., **Invite** is not "ask for the sale."

Invite means:

1. move the patient from observation to participation
2. prepare the doctor entry with shared context
3. preserve trust while increasing decision readiness

If Chapter 6 (Assess & Articulate) answers "what is happening,"
Chapter 7 answers "what do we do with this now?"

Invite is the bridge from understanding to action.

---

## Why Handoffs Leak

Most handoff failures come from one of five patterns:

1. **vague transitions**
   - "Doctor will check."
2. **authority collapse**
   - hygienist steps out of the conversation too early
3. **patient passivity**
   - no invitation to engage with options
4. **doctor cold-start**
   - doctor enters without orientation and restarts from zero
5. **time-pressure language**
   - rushed tone signals "get through this" instead of "let's decide well"

These leaks are operational, not personality flaws.
Which is good news:
what is operational can be trained.

---

## The Invite Sequence

Use this four-step micro-sequence before doctor exam for significant findings:

1. **Pre-Frame**
2. **Preference Prompt**
3. **Bridge**
4. **Doctor Half-Sentence**

Total time: 15-40 seconds.
Average conversion impact: significant when consistently applied.

---

## Step 1: Pre-Frame

Pre-Frame tells the patient what is about to happen and why.

Script template:

> "When Dr. ___ comes in, I want us to look at this area together so you can decide the best next step while it is still manageable."

Why it works:

- sets expectation for joint review
- reinforces urgency without pressure
- frames decision as collaborative

Without pre-frame, doctor entry feels abrupt and evaluative.
With pre-frame, doctor entry feels like continuation.

---

## Step 2: Preference Prompt

This restores agency before the doctor enters.

Script template:

> "When we review options, do you want to start with what fixes this soonest, or what is easiest to phase with your schedule?"

Why it works:

- patient has role in the conversation
- resistance lowers because control is visible
- doctor can tailor options to declared preference

This is not manipulation.
It is informed-choice setup.

---

## Step 3: Bridge

The Bridge is spoken to doctor in front of patient.
It transfers context, finding, and orientation in one concise line.

Bridge template:

> "Dr. ___, we reviewed [finding/location] on camera. [Patient] is most interested in [preference]. Can you walk options with us?"

Bridge quality criteria:

- specific finding and location
- evidence mention
- patient preference included
- clear ask to doctor

No details dump.
No vague "has questions."

---

## Step 4: Doctor Half-Sentence

This is the doctor's first response to the bridge.

Weak doctor entry:
> "Okay, open."

Strong doctor entry:
> "Great, thanks. I see exactly what you are describing at #19, and we can look at options that fit your schedule."

That first half-sentence confirms continuity and protects trust.

When doctor enters cold, the handoff leaks.
When doctor enters connected, acceptance rises.

## The Three Invite Modes

Not every case requires the same invitation style.

### Mode 1: Preventive Invite
*(early finding, low urgency, high teachable moment)*

Use when:
- early lesions
- wear patterns
- mild progression indicators

Goal:
build action mindset before urgency spikes.

### Mode 2: Protective Invite
*(moderate progression, clear risk if delayed)*

Use when:
- fractured cusp risk
- recurring decay with structural vulnerability
- progressing periodontal findings

Goal:
prevent avoidable escalation.

### Mode 3: Priority Invite
*(high concern, accelerated risk timeline)*

Use when:
- likely acute progression risk
- soft tissue concern requiring rapid referral
- findings where delay meaningfully changes complexity

Goal:
move from discussion to immediate next-step commitment.

If teams only use one invitation style, they either over-escalate small findings or under-escalate meaningful ones.

## Language That Leaks at Invite Stage

Avoid these phrases in significant finding conversations:

- "Let's just have doctor take a peek."
- "It might be nothing."
- "You can think about it forever."
- "Insurance probably won't cover this anyway."
- "We can revisit in six months." (without clear risk framing)

Each one lowers urgency, authority, or agency.

Replace with:

- clear relevance
- shared review
- options framing
- timeline honesty

---

## Invite Under Doctor Delay Pressure

Reality: doctors run behind.
Invite must still hold when exam timing slips.

When delay exceeds 5-7 minutes, use a "hold the momentum" script:

> "Dr. ___ is finishing up and will join us shortly. While we wait, I want to make sure we are clear on what we saw and what questions you want answered first."

Then ask:

1. "What matters most to you in fixing this?"
2. "Any concerns about timing or cost you want us to address first?"

This turns dead wait into prepared decision time.

Do not let delay collapse invitation quality.

---

## Invite and Financial Sensitivity

A common fear:
"If I invite too directly, patients will feel sold to."

Invite is not financial pressure.
Invite is decision readiness.

Financial sensitivity sequence:

1. confirm clinical recommendation
2. clarify patient preference
3. then discuss benefit/financing logistics

Keep this order.
If you lead with cost, patients evaluate price before value.
If you lead with value, patients evaluate cost in context.

---

## Team Choreography: Handoff Standards

For Invite to scale across a team, define a standard:

### Standard A: Hygienist Pre-Exam Checklist

- finding named
- visual evidence shown
- patient preference captured
- bridge sentence prepared

### Standard B: Doctor Entry Standard

- acknowledge bridge
- confirm finding location
- align with preference
- present options

### Standard C: Post-Exam Re-Entry

After doctor exits, hygienist re-engages:

> "What feels clearest now, and what do you want to schedule first?"

Invite is incomplete without re-entry.
Otherwise momentum leaks at checkout.

## Coaching Invite in 4 Weeks

### Week 1

- baseline current handoff language
- identify top three leak phrases

### Week 2

- train four-step Invite sequence
- role-play 10 encounters/day in huddle blocks

### Week 3

- audit live handoffs
- score bridge quality (0-5)

### Week 4

- compare SDA before/after
- calibrate doctor half-sentence consistency

One note:
if doctor behavior is inconsistent, hygienist invite gains stall.
Coaching must include both roles.

## Metrics for This Chapter

Track these to validate Invite implementation:

- same-day acceptance (SDA) from hygiene findings
- bridge quality score
- doctor cold-start frequency
- delay-to-decision intervals
- patient "felt involved in decision" comments

Expected early wins:

- shorter time from finding to scheduled treatment
- less repetitive explaining by doctors

If SDA rises but "felt pressured" comments rise too:
adjust tone and preference prompts.

The target is decisive and respectful, not forceful.

## Tool 7.3 - Delay Recovery Protocol

> *Use when doctor exam delays threaten conversion quality.*

### Trigger

- Doctor delay exceeds 5 minutes after intended handoff point.

### Hygienist Script

> "Dr. ___ is finishing up and will be right in. While we wait, let's make sure your top question gets answered first. Is your biggest concern timing, comfort, or long-term durability?"

### Recovery Steps

1. confirm top concern
2. restate finding in plain language
3. note preference for doctor
4. deliver updated bridge on doctor entry

### Updated Bridge Template

> "Dr. ___, while we waited we clarified that [patient] is most concerned about [priority]. We reviewed [finding] at [location] and they want options aligned with that."

### Post-Visit Review

- delay length: ______ minutes
- conversion outcome: [ ] scheduled [ ] undecided [ ] declined
- any process change needed? _______________________
